
════ QUALITY (independent of latency) ════

chrF++ against the official verbatim record. Higher is better.
chrF++ is the translation metric: it scores character n-gram overlap,
so it survives legitimate paraphrase and works the same in every script.

It is primary here, and WER/CER are secondary, for two concrete reasons:
  - computeWER() falls back to PROPORTIONAL CHUNKING above 3,000 words,
    comparing chunk i of the reference to chunk i of the hypothesis. Any
    drift between them inflates the score, so WER on the 82-minute
    session is an approximation. chrF++ has no such cutoff.
  - Chinese has no word boundaries, so word-level WER is noise, and its
    CER exceeded 100% on one cell — an artifact, not a measurement.

NTR — the model broadcasters actually certify live subtitles with.
Errors are weighted by how much MEANING they destroy (minor 0.25,
standard 0.5, serious 1.0) instead of counted equally, and split by
origin: translation error vs speech-recognition error. The accepted
broadcast threshold is 98.

⚠ SUPPRESSED BELOW 80% COVERAGE ('cov' in the cell). NTR's denominator
  is the candidate's OWN word count and its windows are proportional
  slices, so a system emitting 40% of the meeting is graded on 40% of
  the material against a reference slice it does not correspond to.
  Measured: the Deepgram-multi pipeline scores 96.6 on French while its
  output is nonsense at 41% coverage. NTR answers 'how good were the
  subtitles that appeared' — a real question, but meaningless without
  coverage, and actively misleading when coverage is low.

════ CAPTION READABILITY ════

A stream of correct words is not captioning. Live-subtitle practice
judges whether a viewer can READ the result: how much text lands at
once and how fast it is replaced. ~21 chars/sec is the usual adult
ceiling; above it, line one is still being read when line two arrives.

'segmented: no' means the system emits token fragments, not caption
units — it is not a captioning system, and anyone shipping it as one
must add segmentation, line-breaking and timing themselves.

Systems run before this metric existed have no row here; that is a
data gap, not a pass. Soniox and OpenAI Realtime both emit token
fragments and would report 'segmented: no' if re-run.
